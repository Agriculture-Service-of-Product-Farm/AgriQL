How AgriQL differs

A spreadsheet and a general-purpose database will both hold farm tables. AgriQL is narrower. The differences below are what version 3.10.1 actually does. They are not a claim that AgriQL is faster, or that it replaces every other system.

SpreadsheetGeneral-purpose databaseAgriQL 3.10.1
What a number isA number or textA number or textA measurement. Incompatible units are rejected.
Millimetres plus kilogramsAllowedStored, and added if you askRejected. They are not the same kind of quantity.
Millimetres plus metresYou convert themYou convert themAccepted. Both are length.
A season of recordsOften several workbooksA server, or a file with no agricultural meaningOne .aqdb file.
A summaryFormulas across cellsSQL, including joins of many tablesFilter and aggregate one table: count, minimum, maximum, average, standard deviation.
Joining tablesManualYesNot in this release.
Excel, CSV, JSONExcel is the fileDepends on the productCSV, TSV, JSON, and Excel can be imported and exported.
Parquet, or a sheet that lives only onlineNoSometimesNot accepted.
Empty values and odd numbersYou lookYou write the checkPROFILE reports them. SERIES … ON counts gaps in a time column.
Python and RThey read the sheetThey use a driverThey return rows from AgriQL. They are not a second database.

Choose a general-purpose database when you need joins, many applications, or a format AgriQL does not read. Choose AgriQL when the risk is mixing up agricultural measurements, or losing the season across files that no longer agree.
